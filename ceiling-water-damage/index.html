Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Las Cruces Homeowners Should Know

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

Warning Signs

  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Water Travels Sideways

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Older Ceiling Materials

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.
