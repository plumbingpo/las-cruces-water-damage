Appliance Leak Water Damage in Las Cruces: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Las Cruces, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.
