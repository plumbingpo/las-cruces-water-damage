Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Las Cruces

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Las Cruces homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Hidden Mold Growth

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Flooring Damage

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • Whether mold is present and needs containment and removal before drying can finish.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.

Our Process

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.
