Crawl Space Water Removal: What Las Cruces Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.
