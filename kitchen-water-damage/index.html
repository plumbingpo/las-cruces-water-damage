Kitchen Water Damage in Las Cruces

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Las Cruces crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Under the Base Cabinets

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

What We Do

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
