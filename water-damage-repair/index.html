A dry house is not yet a finished house. Once the moisture readings are back to normal, there are still cut walls, missing flooring and bare subfloor to deal with. The repair phase is where reconstruction happens. Our team in Las Cruces replaces what could not be saved and matches it to what was there before, so the room looks like a room again and not a job site.

No two repair jobs are the same size. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. On a large one it can mean insulation, full sheets of drywall, subfloor, hardwood or laminate, cabinet boxes, trim and doors. We replace materials like for like where possible, and we tell you plainly when an exact match is not available so you can choose.

We do not close anything in while it is still damp. A moisture meter has to show normal levels in the subfloor and framing before new material is installed. Cover wood that is still damp, and mold grows unseen behind the new paint. You also receive a written scope and schedule ahead of the work, which spells out what gets rebuilt and when.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.
  • Paint is bubbling, peeling or stained brown where water ran down the wall.
  • The subfloor gives or feels spongy underfoot where water once stood.
  • Trim around doors and along the floor is bowed and will not lie flat.
  • Water under the tile left cracked grout lines or tiles that rock when stepped on.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • File the written scope and photos of the completed repair with your household papers.
  • Caulk around tubs, showers and sinks yearly so water stays out of the wall.
  • Future repairs match better when you have held on to extra flooring, tile and the paint information.
  • Take pictures of damaged materials prior to removal, since the insurer may want them later.
  • Curing time for paint, floor adhesive and caulk should pass before the room sees water or hard use.
