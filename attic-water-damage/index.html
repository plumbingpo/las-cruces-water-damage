Attic Water Damage: What Las Cruces Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. For Las Cruces homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
Our Process

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

The roof or ventilation issue behind it is pointed out to you, and we are glad to pass what we found to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

A Falling Ceiling

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.
